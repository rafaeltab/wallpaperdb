---
title: "ADR-001: Fastify over NestJS"
description: Why the project retained Fastify and TSyringe instead of migrating to NestJS.
---

Accepted on 2025-01-20 as the project expanded beyond Ingestor. This records the framework choice at that time; later context decisions govern service composition, including [Effect in Gateway](/docs/architecture/decisions/gateway-effect) and [Asset](/docs/architecture/decisions/asset-effect).

## Decision

Keep Fastify and the existing TSyringe composition rather than migrate to NestJS.

## Rationale

Ingestor already had reusable connection managers, error handling, OpenTelemetry setup, state-machine logic, and TesterBuilder test infrastructure. NestJS offered more conventions, generated Swagger documentation, and a larger community, but those benefits did not justify rewriting the working service.

The original estimates put shared-package extraction and a second Fastify service at three weeks, versus five to seven weeks with a NestJS migration. These were planning estimates, not measured delivery results.

## Alternatives

- NestJS would reduce manual registration but require a four-to-six-week Ingestor migration, retraining, and regression testing.
- tRPC offered end-to-end types for TypeScript clients but did not address the project's multi-service composition needs.
- Hono offered no identified advantage over the existing minimal framework.

## Consequences

The project retained its existing tests and framework-independent business logic. It also retained responsibility for route registration, documentation, and consistency between services. Shared packages and templates were the planned way to reduce repeated setup.

The original reconsideration triggers were a team larger than five developers, ten or more services, more complex orchestration, or difficulty hiring for the stack. A later migration could wrap shared packages in NestJS modules while replacing route handlers, dependency injection, and registration. The original estimate was two to three weeks per service.

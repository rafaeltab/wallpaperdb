# Backend NATS and JetStream

Scope: backend NATS adapters and their JetStream publishers, consumers, subjects, and stream configuration. Links beside the guidance explain the underlying NATS behavior and its limits. General [event contracts and delivery guarantees](distributed-interactions.md) continue to apply.

## Consumers and parallel work

- Prefer durable pull consumers for reliable work that needs horizontal scaling, controlled flow, and error handling. Workers performing the same processing responsibility share a durable consumer; independent processing responsibilities use separate consumers to receive their own copies. [NATS consumer guidance](https://github.com/nats-io/nats.docs/blob/master/nats-concepts/jetstream/consumers.md), [worker pools](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/worker-pool).
- Use explicit acknowledgements when completion must be tracked per message. NATS ordered consumers are ephemeral, unacknowledged readers intended for inspection and sequential reading; use an acknowledged durable consumer for restartable business processing. [Ordered consumers](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/ordered-consumer).
- Bound pull batches, client buffering, and active handlers to processing capacity. `MaxAckPending` bounds outstanding unacknowledged messages across all clients of one consumer; account for that shared scope when scaling workers. Ordinary parallel consumption does not require partitioning. [Pull limits](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/pull-consumers#the-two-fields-that-bound-a-pull), [shared pending-ack limits](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/pull-consumers#pitfalls).

## Processing in order by entity

- Where correctness requires processing in order for an entity or aggregate, use deterministic subject partitioning by that stable key to retain parallelism between partitions. Put the key in the subject tokens used by the transform. Ensure all event types that must be serialized together reach the same partition consumer. This implements the [application's serialization scope](distributed-interactions.md#delivery-reliability). [NATS subject partitioning](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/subject-mapping#rewrite-subjects-on-the-way-in), [partitioned consumption](https://nats.io/blog/orbit-partitioned-consumer-groups/).
- A straightforward arrangement uses one filtered durable pull consumer per partition and processing responsibility, shared by worker replicas, with explicit acknowledgements and `MaxAckPending = 1`. Different partitions process concurrently; unrelated keys in one partition also wait for each other. This is a supported arrangement, not the only NATS topology: partitioned consumer groups can assign several partitions to a member. [NATS partitioned consumer groups](https://nats.io/blog/orbit-partitioned-consumer-groups/).
- Add or remove worker replicas without changing the partition mapping; useful parallelism remains bounded by the partitions. Changing the partition count or key changes routing, and changing a transform does not rewrite stored messages. Account for existing assignments and retained messages when changing the mapping; a transform also changes the subjects consumers must filter. [Subject-transform pitfalls](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/subject-mapping#pitfalls).
- Keep [replay-safe effects and ordering checks](distributed-interactions.md#delivery-reliability). A pending-ack limit does not lock external resources: acknowledgement timeout can redeliver work while its earlier attempt continues. Partitioning also cannot establish a business order across independent producers merely by grouping their messages. [Redelivery behavior](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/acknowledgment#pitfalls), [NATS ordering guarantees](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/delivery-and-acknowledgment).

## Publishing

- Use JetStream publishing and await a successful `PubAck` before reporting that a message was stored or marking its outbox publication complete. Inspect every result when publishing concurrently. A timeout leaves storage uncertain; a publish acknowledgement confirms storage, not consumer processing. [Publishing and acknowledgements](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/publishing).
- Give retryable publications a stable `Nats-Msg-Id`, unique to the intended message within its destination stream, and reuse it on retry. Map the [event occurrence identity](distributed-interactions.md#contracts) into that ID without collisions between producers. Deduplication has a configured window and does not replace consumer idempotency. [Duplicate suppression](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/publishing#avoiding-duplicate-writes).

## Acknowledgements, retry, and quarantine

- Set acknowledgement deadlines for the processing duration, including time messages spend buffered after delivery. Send in-progress acknowledgements while legitimate long-running work continues. If `BackOff` is configured, its first duration supplies the initial acknowledgement deadline. [Acknowledgement timers and backoff](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/acknowledgment#backoff-a-growing-delay-between-attempts).
- Use delayed negative acknowledgements for delayed application retries: a bare `nak()` requests immediate redelivery, and consumer `BackOff` controls acknowledgement timeouts rather than negative acknowledgements. [Delayed negative acknowledgements](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/acknowledgment#negative-ack-with-a-delay).
- Implement the existing [completion, finite-retry, and durable-quarantine guarantees](distributed-interactions.md#delivery-reliability) explicitly. For permanent or exhausted failures, confirm durable quarantine acceptance before `ack()` or `term()`; a JetStream quarantine publication needs its successful `PubAck`. `term()` stops redelivery, and reaching `MaxDeliver` stops further attempts and emits an advisory; neither creates a dead-letter queue. Messages exhausted at `MaxDeliver` remain in the stream subject to retention. Account for that path in quarantine handling, including failures that prevent a handler from running. [Acknowledgement behavior](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/acknowledgment), [NATS delivery limits](https://github.com/nats-io/nats.docs/blob/master/nats-concepts/jetstream/consumers.md).

## Retention and replay

- Use `LimitsPolicy` when history must survive consumer acknowledgements for late consumers or replay. Choose age, count, and byte limits to meet the required replay horizon; limits-based retention is not unlimited retention. [Limits retention](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/retention-policies).
- Use `InterestPolicy` only when retaining messages for the currently interested consumers meets the contract; messages without matching consumer interest are removed. Use `WorkQueuePolicy` for work assigned to one processing responsibility: overlapping consumer filters are prohibited, so it does not provide independent fan-out over the same subjects. [Interest and work-queue retention](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/retention-policies).
- Account for retention limits under every policy, including removal of unprocessed messages. Choose overflow behavior deliberately: `DiscardOld` evicts older messages; `DiscardNew` rejects new publications when the applicable capacity limit is reached. [Retention limits](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/retention-policies), [stream limits and discard behavior](https://github.com/nats-io/nats.docs/blob/master/nats-concepts/jetstream/streams.md).

## Shutdown and testing

- Apply [bounded lifecycle ownership](composition-and-operations.md#configuration-and-lifecycle) to pull loops, handlers, and the connection. During graceful shutdown, stop taking new work, allow in-flight handlers to finish within that bound, send their required acknowledgements, and await publication results before closing. Drain does not acknowledge JetStream work for the application; flush does not confirm JetStream storage. [NATS drain and shutdown](https://docs.nats.io/learn/resilient-clients/drain-and-shutdown).

Use the existing [adapter contract tests](adapters.md#testing) with real NATS for broker-dependent guarantees. Application decisions stay in [capability tests](application-and-domain.md#testing), and telemetry follows its [separate testing policy](composition-and-operations.md#testing).

What the range controls mean
  • OKLab radius: includes shades within a perceptual distance of the anchor. The control uses the scale where black-to-white is about 100. Radius 20 means distance 0.20 in OKLab; it does not mean “20% similar.”
  • RGB box: independently limits red, green, and blue channel differences from the anchor. Values are percentage-point differences, not relative changes.
  • HSL box: limits hue, saturation, and lightness differences. Hue uses the shortest direction around the color wheel; ±180° allows every hue.
  • HSV box: limits hue, saturation, and value (brightness). A black anchor with all hues, saturation ±100 points, and value ±25 points accepts dark colors of any hue. It is broader than dark gray.
  • Neutral anchors: black, gray, and white have no defined hue. HSL and HSV ranges around them must allow all hues; saturation controls how neutral the accepted shades remain.
  • Combined constraints: adding a second constraint uses the same anchor. A shade must satisfy both constraints. For example, use HSV for brightness and RGB to exclude a particular channel.
  • Overlapping ranges: requested percentages describe distinct portions. The solver distributes shared colors across those portions and cannot use one pixel twice. Raw available areas can therefore total more than 100%, while allocations cannot.
  • Exact proportions: excess accepted color in the unrequested remainder is penalized. Minimum mode allows the remainder to contain extra accepted colors.
  • Center preference: the default ranks composition error first, then prefers shades closer to the anchor among equally good compositions. Preference is 100% at the center and 50% at the boundary; a boundary shade still counts fully toward the literal image area.
  • Sample swatches: show examples accepted by the hard range, not every accepted shade. The comparison methods use that same region. The optional outside-softening method additionally gives nearby outside shades partial credit and can trade area accuracy for shade similarity.
  • Palette approximation: all area estimates come from representative palette colors. A hard boundary near a palette centroid can hide smaller variations in the original image.
